# PIF suggester: evaluation against the hand-coded CSB incidents

Generated by `tools/evaluate_suggester.py`; do not hand-edit. Method, rules and caveats: `docs/suggester.html`.

Data: 23 coded investigations, 67 (investigation, PIF) gold pairs over 10 distinct PIFs, split into two cohorts by `cohort` in `csb_investigations.csv`: **development** (10 investigations, 25 pairs, coded before the rules were written, so the rule author had read these quotes) and **held-out** (13 investigations, 42 pairs, coded after the rules in `crosswalk/pif_rules.csv` were committed; the rules were not changed afterwards). The narrative for each investigation is the concatenation of the quoted CSB passages used to code it.

**Read this first.** The input text is the evidence the gold labels were coded from, so recall measures whether the rules' vocabulary reaches the words a coder already pointed at, not whether the suggester finds PIFs in a free-running narrative. Precision is measured against an incomplete gold standard (only PIFs with a quoted passage are coded), so some false positives may be real conditions nobody coded. The held-out cohort is a genuine out-of-sample test of the rules for these incidents; the development cohort is resubstitution (optimistic). The same person coded both cohorts and wrote the rules, and the held-out quotes are mostly CSB recommendation wording from summary pages, which differs in register from the development findings. The held-out passages were also chosen by looking for text on each factor's topic (training, procedures, alarms and so on), so their vocabulary is likely to be closer to the rules' keywords than a whole report would be; this inflates recall. Nothing here is an estimate of performance on other incidents; the held-out cohort is the closest thing to one and it is small, so one pair moves a PIF's precision or recall by many points.
